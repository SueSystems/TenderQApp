Legal and operational feasibility studies bridge the gap between "good code" and "usable, compliant business software."

3. Legal Feasibility
This phase assesses whether the proposed system conflicts with legal requirements, data protection acts, or contractual obligations.
•	Data Privacy: The application will strictly adhere to the Kenya Data Protection Act. Personal data—such as contact details for suppliers or officials—that is not in the public domain will be restricted. Access is limited to the company’s designated Contact Person unless documented authorization is provided.
•	Audit Trails: To prevent fraud and resolve disputes, the system is "audit-ready." It maintains a chronological record, categorized by Procurement Entity, to track who approved an award or quote and when. This trail includes all correspondence up until the final certificate of recommendation.
•	Electronic Signatures: To maintain authenticity, digital signatures will not be auto-generated; instead, they must be manually appended by the tender responder.
•	Industry Standards: The module is designed to follow all Public Procurement Laws regarding tender responses in Kenya.

4. Operational Feasibility
This is the "Human Element" of the study—determining if the system fits the organization's culture and workflows.
•	Usability & Adoption: The digital workflow is designed to be more intuitive than manual processes. By clicking on a current tender or quotation, users can view a visual graph indicating pending tasks and the personnel responsible.
•	Stakeholder Buy-in: Because end-users are involved in the development process, the workflow is pre-approved. This ensures alignment with existing business processes, secures management support, and significantly reduces training time

Market Research Update: Risk Assessment
Last week, I tackled the second component of my market research: Resource Allocation. This week, I am focusing on the third component: Risk Assessment.
Risk assessment is about identifying potential "showstoppers" that could render the TenderQApp module unviable, unaffordable, or misaligned with the actual needs it serves—all before a single line of code is written.
As a student Systems Engineer, this is my first time navigating a full SDLC, and I’ve identified the following key risk areas:
1. Operational and Compliance Risks
•	Regulatory/Structural Changes: The risk that government portals (IFMIS, e-GP) change their API structures, authentication, or data formats, potentially rendering my scraping tool obsolete overnight. Navigating the disparity between these government portals and private sector API structures is a significant hurdle.
2. Financial and Project Risks
•	Inaccurate Cost/Time Estimation: The danger of underestimating the maintenance required to keep scraping and response modules functional despite frequent external updates.
•	Budget Overruns: Ensuring my capital allocation framework is robust enough to cover unexpected technical debt or the need for specialized engineering support.
3. Technical Risks
•	Tech Stack Obsolescence: Selecting frameworks or libraries that may lose community support, complicating long-term maintenance and troubleshooting.
My Mitigation Strategy:
1.	Risk Register: I have developed a tracker to categorize risks by Probability (High/Medium/Low) and Business Impact to ensure I stay ahead of potential blockers.
2.	Early Validation: I am validating my core "business logic" by discussing architectural decisions with experienced mentors and professionals in the contracting space.
3.	Feature Prioritization: Using a Value vs. Effort analysis to ruthlessly prioritize "must-have" features, ensuring the initial MVP is lean and high-impact.
This marks the end of my Planning Quality Assurance study and my transition into Code Quality Assurance.

Requirement Gathering—the fundamental reason for building the application- TenderQApp.
1. Integration Requirements (The "Portal" Observation)
•	Observation: Shift from scattered portals to a centralized e-GP.
•	System Requirement: The TenderQApp must provide API integration capabilities or a "Scraper" function to pull data from the National e-GP, IFMIS portals and mails -marketing/quotations automatically.
2. Compliance & Eligibility (The "Special Groups" Observation)
•	Observation: Incorporation of Youth, Women, and PWD.
•	System Requirement: The system must include a Compliance Profile Manager where the firm’s AGPO certificates and special group statuses are stored and automatically appended to relevant bids.
3. Speed & Efficiency (The "5-Day Window" Observation)
•	Observation: Tender windows have shrunk significantly.
•	System Requirement: The app must feature Template Automation. Instead of drafting from scratch, the system should auto-populate standard bid documents (Forms of Tender, Confidential Business Questionnaires, Experience, Personnel, Audited Accounts, Litigation History, SD1 & SD2, Power of Attorney) using stored ERP data to meet tight deadlines.
4. Remote Collaboration (The "Physical Presence" Observation)
•	Observation: Need to reduce reliance on being physically in the office.
•	System Requirement: The module must be Cloud-Based with a Workflow Approval Engine, allowing a manager to review and "e-sign" a bid from a mobile device while the staff is working remotely.
